A service business runs on four tools that don't talk.

A one-van window cleaning company still has to quote, schedule crews, track hours, bill, collect and keep the books. Usually that means a paper diary, a word-processor estimate, a card reader and an evening re-typing everything into QuickBooks.

The usual way

  • Recurring customers live in a diary, and someone has to remember who is due this month
  • Estimates are typed from scratch and emailed as attachments
  • Crews get the day's jobs by phone or text
  • Invoices are written one by one after the work is done
  • Every sale is entered a second time in the accounting software

With The Customer Factor

  • Recurring and one-time jobs sit in a job pool and get dragged onto the schedule
  • Estimates come from templates and go out by email or text
  • Jobs are assigned to an employee or a whole crew, and appear in their app
  • Invoices are created in bulk, and saved cards can pay recurring work automatically
  • Sales flow into QuickBooks Online overnight, or Desktop in a few clicks

Who it's built for

As described by the vendor and its software-directory listings.

Owner-operators and small teams whose revenue is mostly repeat visits to the same addresses. The product is tuned for that rhythm rather than for large field-service fleets.

Every step below happens inside the product. The only thing that leaves it is the finished sale, on its way to the books.

  1. EstimateFrom a template, sent by email or text

What makes it interesting to build.

The hard parts of this kind of product aren't the screens. They are the rules underneath them.

Recurring work as data, not reminders

A window round repeats every 4, 6 or 8 weeks. Modelling frequency, service plans and pricing on the job itself is what lets the job pool fill up on its own.

Two accounting integrations, one source of truth

A scheduled cloud push for QuickBooks Online and a user-triggered sync for Desktop. Both must agree on customers, items and payments, and never post twice.

One record, three audiences

Owners, crews and end customers see the same job with different permissions. The portal shows only what the owner chooses: days, services, history and prices.

Where a next version could go further

  • Navigation and search. The most common complaint in reviews is that records are hard to find.
  • Route optimization. Independent guides note it isn't built in, which matters once a business runs several crews.
  • Message delivery. One reviewer reported client emails not always arriving, a case for delivery tracking.

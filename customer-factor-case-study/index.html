A service business runs on four tools that don't talk.

A small window cleaning company still has to quote, schedule crews, bill, get paid and keep the books. Usually that means a paper diary, a typed estimate, a card reader and an evening re-typing it all into QuickBooks.

The usual way

  • Recurring customers live in a diary, and someone has to remember who is due this month
  • Estimates are typed from scratch and emailed as attachments
  • Crews get the day's jobs by phone call or text
  • Invoices are written one by one after the work is done
  • Every sale is entered a second time in the accounting software

With The Customer Factor

  • Recurring and one-time jobs wait in a job pool and are dragged onto the schedule
  • Estimates come from templates and go out by email or text
  • Jobs are assigned to an employee or a whole crew and show up in their app
  • Invoices are created in bulk; saved cards can pay recurring work automatically
  • Sales flow into QuickBooks Online overnight, or Desktop in a few clicks

One record for the whole business.

The Customer Factor keeps a service company's customers, jobs, prices and payments together, so the same details are never typed twice.

It's built around repeat visits, which is why it fits trades where the same customers book again and again:

How a job works, start to finish.

One window-cleaning customer, followed through the product.

  1. 1
    Send the estimate

    The office picks a template, adjusts the line items and sends it to the customer by text or email.

  2. 2
    Put it in the job pool

    Once approved, it becomes a job: one-time, or repeating on a set frequency.

  3. 3
    Drag it onto the schedule

    When it's due, the job is dragged onto a day and assigned to an employee or a crew.

  4. 4
    Do the work and track the time

    The crew sees the job in the iOS or Android app and tracks time against it on site.

  5. 5
    Invoice and get paid

    The invoice is paid on the spot by card, check or cash, or a saved card is charged for repeat work.

  6. 6
    Land in the books

    The payment flows into QuickBooks with the rest of the day's work.

An estimate becomes a job in one tap; the invoice is paid on site.

Technically, what makes it interesting.

What sits underneath the screens, and what it saves a service business every week.

Repeat work that schedules itself

Each job keeps its own frequency and price. When it's due again it comes back ready to schedule, so no repeat customer is forgotten.

Real hours, without timesheets

Crews tap travel, arrival, start and finish on each job, and clock-ins and routes are logged per employee, so the owner sees real hours worked.

One job record for everyone

The office, the crew's app and the customer portal all use the same job. Customers only see what the owner chooses: bookable days, services, past jobs and prices.

Books that stay up to date

Works with QuickBooks Online and QuickBooks Desktop, with the sync included in the subscription. Card payments and email and text messages are built in, so nothing is typed twice.
